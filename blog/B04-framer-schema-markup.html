If you have ever searched for your competitor in Google and noticed their result showing star ratings, FAQ dropdowns directly in the SERP, or a carousel of recent articles with rich preview images, you have seen schema markup at work. These enhanced search results, called rich results, do not happen by accident. They require structured data injected into the HTML of your pages, telling Google precisely what kind of content you are publishing.

The challenge for Framer users is straightforward and frustrating: Framer has no native schema UI. Unlike WordPress with Yoast SEO or Webflow with its structured data settings, Framer provides no visual form for entering schema data. If you want schema on your Framer site today, you must write raw JSON-LD, wrap it in a script tag, and paste it into your page's custom code head. For most designers and founders using Framer, that process is either completely opaque or error-prone enough to produce broken markup.

This guide covers everything: what schema markup is, why it matters, every type relevant to Framer sites, how to add it manually, and how to automate the entire process without touching a single line of JSON.

What is Schema Markup and Why Does It Matter?

Schema markup is structured data written in a format called JSON-LD (JavaScript Object Notation for Linked Data). It lives inside a <script type="application/ld+json"> tag in your page's HTML and tells search engines not just what your page says, but what your content is. There is a meaningful difference between those two things.

Without schema, Google reads your page like a document and tries to infer meaning from text and context. With schema, you are explicitly stating: "This page is an Article, written by this author, published on this date, about this topic, belonging to this organization." Google no longer has to guess.

What Rich Results Can Schema Unlock?

The most tangible benefit of schema is eligibility for rich results. Depending on the schema types you implement, Google may display your content with visual enhancements in the SERP, including:

  • FAQ dropdowns directly beneath your search result, expanding to show question and answer pairs
  • Star ratings and review counts for products, recipes, and local businesses
  • Article rich results with headline, image, author, and publication date in news carousels
  • How-To step previews showing numbered steps within the SERP
  • Sitelinks search box on branded queries, allowing users to search your site from within Google
  • Breadcrumb trails replacing the URL in your search result with a clean navigation path
  • Event listings with date, time, and location shown inline

Rich results consistently improve click-through rates. Studies from major SEO platforms have found that FAQ-enriched results can increase CTR by 20 to 30 percent compared to standard blue-link results. That CTR improvement signals relevance to Google, which has indirect effects on ranking over time.

Does schema directly improve rankings? Schema markup is not a direct ranking factor in the traditional sense. Google has confirmed it does not boost rankings purely from presence. However, it enables rich results, and rich results improve CTR, and improved CTR does influence how Google evaluates a page's relevance and authority. The relationship is real, just indirect.

The Technical Format: JSON-LD

There are three formats for structured data: JSON-LD, Microdata, and RDFa. Google recommends JSON-LD because it lives separately from your HTML content in a single script block, making it easier to add, modify, and validate without touching your visible page code. Framer's custom code injection supports JSON-LD perfectly.

A minimal Article schema looks like this:

<script type="application/ld+json"> { "@context": "https://schema.org", "@type": "Article", "headline": "How to Add Schema Markup to Framer", "author": { "@type": "Person", "name": "Jane Smith" }, "datePublished": "2026-05-03", "publisher": { "@type": "Organization", "name": "7 Seers", "logo": { "@type": "ImageObject", "url": "https://rankframe.com/logo.png" } } } </script>
