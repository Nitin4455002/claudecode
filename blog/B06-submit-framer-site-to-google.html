How to Submit Your Framer Site to Google: The Complete Guide

By 7 Seers Team  |  May 3, 2026  |  14 min read

Building a Framer site is only half the work. Until Google knows your pages exist and indexes them, your content will not appear in search results regardless of how well-optimized it is. This guide walks through every step of getting your Framer site into Google: verification, sitemap submission, URL-level indexing via the Google Indexing API, and how to confirm that pages are actually indexed. Each method is covered both manually and via RankFrame for the fastest possible workflow.

Why Active Submission Matters for Framer Sites

Google discovers new web pages primarily through two mechanisms: crawling links from already-indexed pages, and reading sitemaps submitted to Google Search Console. For a brand-new Framer site with no inbound links, Googlebot may not discover your pages for weeks or even months without active submission.

Even for established sites, passive discovery is unreliable for new content. If you publish a new blog post in Framer today and do nothing, Googlebot might crawl it tomorrow or it might take three weeks. The frequency and priority of Googlebot crawls depend on your domain's crawl budget, which is influenced by site age, authority, and historical update frequency.

The Three Levels of Google Submission

1
Search Console Verification Tells Google you own the site and enables GSC data reporting. Required before any other submission step.
2
Sitemap Submission Submits a machine-readable list of all your URLs to Google. Google crawls them on its own schedule -- typically within days to a week for new sites.
3
Google Indexing API (URL Push) Directly notifies Google of specific new or updated URLs, triggering priority crawling. The fastest submission method -- often effective within hours.

All three steps are covered in detail below. For the fastest results, complete all three in sequence.

1

Verify Your Framer Site in Google Search Console

Google Search Console (GSC) is the central hub for managing your site's relationship with Google Search. Before you can submit sitemaps or use any submission tools, you need to verify site ownership. Framer supports verification via an HTML meta tag, which is the most straightforward method.

Get the Verification Meta Tag from GSC

1

Go to Google Search Console and sign in with the Google account you want to use for your site.

2

Click Add Property. Choose URL prefix and enter your Framer site's full URL (e.g., https://yoursite.com).

3

On the verification screen, select HTML tag as your verification method. You will see a meta tag that looks like this:

<meta name="google-site-verification" content="YOUR_VERIFICATION_CODE_HERE" />

Copy the entire meta tag.

Paste the Tag into Framer Site Settings

4

In the Framer editor, click the Site Settings icon (gear icon at the top right of the canvas). This opens Site Settings, which applies to all pages.

5

Scroll down to Custom Code. In the Start of <head> tag field (or Head field, depending on your Framer version), paste the verification meta tag.

6

Click Publish in the Framer editor to make the tag live. The tag must be on the published site -- Framer's preview URLs will not work for verification.

📸 Screenshot: Framer Site Settings panel showing the Custom Code Head field with a Google verification meta tag pasted in

Complete Verification in GSC

7

Return to Google Search Console and click Verify. GSC will fetch your homepage and confirm the meta tag is present. If successful, you will see a "Ownership verified" confirmation.

Do not remove the verification tag after verifying GSC periodically re-checks for the verification tag. If you remove it from Framer Site Settings, Google may revoke your verification and you will lose access to GSC data for your property.

Once verified, it typically takes 24-48 hours for GSC to begin populating data for your property, including any coverage issues, performance data, and crawl stats.

2

Submit Your Framer Sitemap

Framer automatically generates a sitemap at yourdomain.com/sitemap.xml. This XML file lists every published page on your site, including CMS collection item pages, in a format Googlebot can read and prioritize. Submitting it to GSC tells Google exactly where all your content lives.

Find Your Framer Sitemap URL

Your Framer sitemap is always located at https://yourdomain.com/sitemap.xml. Before submitting, visit this URL in a browser to confirm it exists and loads correctly. You should see an XML document listing your pages. If it returns a 404, check that your Framer site is published to a custom domain (not a .framer.website subdomain, which also has a sitemap at the same path).

What does Framer's sitemap include? Framer's auto-generated sitemap includes all published static pages and all CMS collection item pages. It does not include pages set to noindex, password-protected pages, or draft pages. The sitemap is updated automatically every time you publish.

Submit the Sitemap in Google Search Console

1

In Google Search Console, select your verified Framer property from the left-side property selector.

2

In the left navigation, under Indexing, click Sitemaps.

3

In the Add a new sitemap field, type sitemap.xml (GSC prefills your domain). Click Submit.

4

GSC will display the sitemap in the Submitted Sitemaps list. After a few minutes, refresh the page -- you should see the status change from Pending to Success, along with a count of discovered URLs.

📸 Screenshot: Google Search Console Sitemaps panel showing yourdomain.com/sitemap.xml with status "Success" and URL count

Sitemap Submission Timelines

After submitting a sitemap, Google typically begins crawling the listed URLs within a few hours to a few days. Full indexing of all URLs -- especially for larger sites with many CMS pages -- can take one to three weeks for a new domain, or one to seven days for an established domain. Sitemap submission is a necessary step but not the fastest path to indexing individual pages.

For immediate indexing of specific high-priority pages, proceed to Step 3.

3

Push Individual URLs Using the Google Indexing API

The Google Indexing API is a direct channel to Google's crawling infrastructure. Instead of waiting for Googlebot to discover and crawl your pages, you send Google a signal saying "this URL is new or updated, please crawl it now." Google treats these notifications as high-priority crawl requests and typically processes them within hours.

This is the fastest available method for getting Framer pages into Google's index, and it is especially valuable for time-sensitive content: a new landing page before a campaign launch, a new blog post you want indexed the same day, or updated pricing pages after a product change.

The Manual Method: GSC URL Inspection

Without RankFrame, you can submit individual URLs via the URL Inspection tool in Google Search Console:

1

In GSC, click the search bar at the top (labeled "Inspect any URL in...") and enter the full URL of the page you want to index.

2

GSC fetches and analyzes the URL. Click Request Indexing in the URL Inspection result panel.

3

Repeat for every URL you want to prioritize. The GSC URL Inspection tool limits you to a small number of requests per day, so it is impractical for bulk submission.

GSC URL Inspection rate limits The Request Indexing button in GSC is rate-limited to approximately 10-20 requests per day per property. For sites with many new or updated pages, this is a significant bottleneck. The Google Indexing API accessed via RankFrame has a much higher quota and supports bulk submission in a single action.

4

Confirm Indexing Status

Submitting a URL does not guarantee indexing. Google may crawl a URL and still choose not to index it if it detects thin content, duplicate content, a noindex tag, or other quality issues. Confirming actual indexing is a separate step from submission.

Manual Confirmation: GSC URL Inspection

For any URL, you can check its index status in Google Search Console by pasting the URL into the Inspect URL search bar. The result shows one of these statuses:

StatusMeaningAction
URL is on Google The page is indexed and eligible to appear in search results No action needed
URL is not on Google Google has not indexed the page yet; may be pending crawl or has an issue Check for crawl errors; resubmit if recently published
Discovered, not indexed Google found the URL but chose not to index it; typically a quality or noindex issue Check for noindex tag, thin content, or duplicate content signals

The Check Live button queries Google's index in real time and displays whether the URL shows as "Submitted and indexed" without requiring you to open GSC.

Common Indexing Issues on Framer Sites (and How to Fix Them)

Even after completing all submission steps, some pages may fail to index. The following issues are specific to Framer sites and represent the most common causes of indexing failure.

1. Noindex Tag Accidentally Applied

Symptom: GSC URL Inspection shows "Excluded by noindex tag" or "Crawled -- currently not indexed" with a noindex reason.

Cause in Framer: If you set a page to noindex in Framer's page settings (typically done during development to prevent staging pages from being indexed), that setting persists to the published site. Check each page's SEO settings under the page info panel.

Fix: Open the page settings in Framer, scroll to the SEO section, and confirm the noindex toggle is off. Republish and resubmit the URL.

2. Framer Preview Domain Indexed Instead of Custom Domain

Symptom: Your content appears in Google, but the indexed URL is yoursite.framer.website instead of your custom domain.

Cause: Framer sites are also accessible at the .framer.website subdomain by default. If Google discovered and indexed this domain first, it may prefer it as the canonical URL.

Fix: In Framer Site Settings, ensure your custom domain is connected and verify the canonical tag is pointing to your custom domain. RankFrame can verify canonical configuration across your pages via the SEO Audit. Also, verify your custom domain property in GSC separately from the framer.website domain.

3. CMS Collection Pages Not Indexed

Symptom: Your main pages are indexed but CMS blog posts or collection item pages are not appearing in GSC coverage.

Cause: CMS collection pages are included in Framer's sitemap, but Google may crawl them with lower priority if the collection template page itself lacks strong internal link signals.

Fix: Submit your sitemap in GSC if you have not already. Use RankFrame's Submit Indexing feature to push specific CMS page URLs directly to the Indexing API. Ensure your main blog index page links to individual posts (Framer's CMS collection components handle this automatically if configured correctly).

4. Thin or Duplicate Content Signals

Symptom: GSC shows "Discovered -- currently not indexed" or "Crawled -- currently not indexed" for multiple pages.

Cause: Google may determine that the page content is too thin (very short, little unique value) or too similar to another indexed page. This is increasingly common with AI-generated content that lacks originality.

Fix: Expand the page content, add original analysis, images, or data. Use RankFrame's Site Audit to identify pages flagged for thin content. Prioritize indexing your most substantive pages first and build internal links from indexed pages to unindexed ones.

5. Verification Tag Removed After Site Publish

Symptom: GSC sends a "site ownership verification lost" email. You lose access to data and submissions stop working.

Cause: The Google site verification meta tag was removed from Framer Site Settings during a site redesign or settings reset.

Fix: Re-add the verification meta tag to Framer Site Settings head, publish, and re-verify in GSC. Treat the verification tag as permanent infrastructure -- never remove it.

6. URLs Returning Incorrect Status Codes

Symptom: GSC shows crawl errors for specific pages; URL Inspection shows a page error.

Cause: In Framer, pages that have been deleted or unpublished may still appear in the sitemap temporarily, or old URLs that have changed may return 404 errors.

Fix: Use Framer's redirect manager (Pro plan) to create 301 redirects from old URLs to new ones. Monitor 404 errors using RankFrame's 404 Monitor, which tracks broken URLs and helps you prioritize which ones need redirects.

How Often Should You Resubmit?

Resubmission is not a one-time setup. Your Google indexing strategy should be an ongoing part of your Framer publishing workflow. The right frequency depends on how often your content changes.

After Every Publish with New or Updated Content

The most important trigger for resubmission is a Framer publish that includes new pages or meaningfully updated existing pages. Framer updates your sitemap on every publish, but Google does not recrawl the sitemap instantly. Actively pushing updated URLs via RankFrame's Push URLs feature ensures Google sees your changes within hours rather than days.

Weekly Sitemap Resubmission for Active Content Sites

For blogs or content sites publishing more than a few pieces per week, resubmitting the sitemap in GSC weekly (or confirming it is up to date) is good practice. GSC's Sitemaps section shows when Google last read your sitemap, which helps you identify if Googlebot has stopped crawling it for any reason.
