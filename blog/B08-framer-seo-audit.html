Every serious SEO workflow starts with an audit. You cannot prioritize fixes you cannot see, and you cannot measure progress without a baseline score. For Framer site owners, this presents a problem that is easy to overlook until rankings fail to improve: Framer has no native SEO audit.

Framer gives you a beautiful editor, a fast hosting infrastructure, auto-generated sitemaps, canonical tags, and robots.txt. These are genuine technical SEO advantages. What Framer does not give you is any way to check whether your pages have title tags, whether your H1s are set correctly, whether your images have alt text, whether your canonical tags are pointing to the right URLs, or whether your page speed is actively hurting your rankings. The editor does not warn you when SEO fundamentals are missing, and there is no analysis panel, scoring system, or report generator anywhere in the native Framer interface.

The result is that many Framer sites are published with correctable SEO problems that persist for months because the site owner simply does not know they exist. This guide explains exactly what a Framer SEO audit should cover, how to run one using RankFrame, how to prioritize the results, and how to produce a PDF report for clients or internal stakeholders.

Why Framer Has No Native Audit and Why That Matters

Framer is a design and development platform. Its core value proposition is visual — a canvas that turns design into production-ready websites. SEO is a secondary concern in Framer's product roadmap. The platform provides the technical infrastructure (sitemap, robots.txt, canonical tags) but leaves on-page SEO analysis entirely to the site owner.

Framer Analytics, which is built into the platform, shows you visitor counts, pageviews, and bounce rate. This is traffic data, not search performance data. It tells you how many people arrived and whether they stayed, but not what keywords they searched, what position you rank for those queries, or why certain pages receive no organic traffic at all.

For keyword and ranking data, Framer users need to connect Google Search Console separately, and even then, GSC shows you aggregate query performance after the fact. It does not tell you what is wrong with your page's SEO setup or what to fix.

The gap this creates is practical and consequential. A designer launches a Framer site for a client. The site looks excellent. It loads quickly. But six weeks later, organic traffic is flat. The designer checks Framer Analytics: traffic exists, mostly from direct and referral sources, very little from organic search. The designer opens the Framer editor and sees no warnings, no flags, no suggestions. Without a dedicated audit tool, there is no immediate way to know that six pages are missing meta descriptions, that the homepage has two H1 tags, that none of the 80 images have alt text, or that the blog pages are all canonicalized to the homepage by mistake.

An audit surfaces all of these issues at once, scores them by severity, and gives you a clear action list. RankFrame's SEO Audit does this inside the Framer editor, requiring no external tools, no copying URLs into third-party audit platforms, and no manual spreadsheet tracking.

The Cost of Skipping an Audit Technical SEO errors are invisible until you look for them. Missing title tags, duplicate H1s, broken canonical tags, and missing alt text do not break a website visually. They only damage rankings. Without an audit, these problems can persist for the entire lifecycle of a site.

What a Framer SEO Audit Covers

A comprehensive Framer SEO audit should examine four categories of factors: Basic SEO fundamentals, Advanced SEO technical signals, Performance metrics, and Security checks. Here is what each category contains and why it matters for Framer sites specifically.

Basic SEO

Basic SEO checks are the foundational on-page elements that search engines use to understand and rank your pages. These are the first things Google reads when crawling a page.

Title TagPresence and length check. Titles should be 50-60 characters. Missing or too-long titles hurt click-through rates and keyword relevance.
H1 TagEvery page should have exactly one H1. Multiple H1s confuse page topic signals. Missing H1 means a key ranking signal is absent.
H2 TagsSubheadings help Google understand page structure and support secondary keyword relevance.
Meta DescriptionNot a ranking factor but directly affects click-through rate. Missing descriptions get auto-generated by Google with unpredictable results.
Image Alt TextAll meaningful images should have descriptive alt text. Missing alt text hurts image search visibility and accessibility compliance.
Internal LinksPages without internal links receive less PageRank flow from the rest of the site.
Canonical TagPresence of a self-referential canonical tag prevents duplicate content issues.
Open Graph TagsOG title, description, and image control how your pages appear when shared on social platforms.
Schema MarkupStructured data presence enables rich results in search and provides machine-readable page context.

Advanced SEO

Advanced SEO checks cover technical signals that affect crawlability, indexability, and how Google categorizes and trusts your site.

SitemapPresence and accessibility of your sitemap.xml for crawler discovery.
Robots.txtCorrect configuration that allows Google to crawl your important pages.
Broken Links404 errors from internal or external links waste crawl budget and damage user experience.
Noindex TagsChecks for pages accidentally set to noindex that should be indexed.
Canonical AccuracyVerifies canonical tags point to the correct canonical URLs.
WWW CanonicalizationConfirms consistent www/non-www redirects to prevent duplicate domain indexing.

Performance

Page speed is a confirmed Google ranking factor. Framer's hosting is generally fast, but page-level choices (large unoptimized images, heavy animations, excessive JavaScript) can create performance problems that hurt rankings.

Page SizeTotal page weight check. Large pages load slowly, especially on mobile.
Mobile SpeedLighthouse mobile performance score. Mobile speed is the primary ranking signal for mobile-first indexing.
Desktop SpeedLighthouse desktop performance score for reference and comparison.
Response TimeServer response time (TTFB). High TTFB delays every subsequent page load step.
Image CachingChecks that images are served with appropriate cache headers for browser caching.
Object CountNumber of requests a page makes. High request counts slow load times.

Security

Security checks confirm that your site follows basic security standards that both users and search engines expect.

HTTPSAll pages should be served over HTTPS. HTTP pages receive a "Not Secure" browser warning and a ranking penalty.
Theme VisibilityChecks that your Framer site is not displaying in a way that exposes the editor or preview mode publicly.

Site-Wide SEO Audit with RankFrame

The site-wide audit in RankFrame gives you a single aggregate score across your entire Framer site, along with a filterable breakdown of every issue found. This is your starting point for any SEO work.

Screenshot: RankFrame SEO Audit panel showing the main score panel with a score of 74 out of 100, with filter tabs for All, Green Zone, Red Zone, Warnings, and Speed visible

The Score Panel (0-100)

Your site-wide SEO score is a 0-to-100 number that aggregates the results of all checks across all pages. A higher score means fewer and less severe issues. The score is not a prediction of rankings — it is an index of SEO health. A site with a score of 40 has many unresolved critical issues. A site scoring 90 has minor optimization gaps at most.

The score is divided into three zones. Understanding which zone your score falls in helps you calibrate urgency. For detailed guidance on reading the score, see the Audit Score Panel documentation.

Green Zone

Approximately 85 and above. Few or no critical issues. Your site's SEO fundamentals are in good shape. Focus on optimization rather than remediation.

Warning Zone

Roughly 70 to 84. Moderate issues present that are not causing immediate ranking damage but represent real optimization gaps. Address these after Red Zone items.

Red Zone

Below 70. Critical failures present that are actively limiting crawlability, indexability, or on-page relevance. Fix these immediately.

Filter Tabs: All, Green Zone, Red Zone, Warnings, Speed

The audit panel is filterable by zone. Switching to the Red Zone tab shows only critical failures. This is where your immediate work should be. Every item in the Red Zone is a confirmed SEO problem: a page with no title tag, images across the site with no alt text, a canonical tag pointing to the wrong URL, a page blocked by noindex that should be indexed.

The Warnings tab shows moderate issues: title tags that are technically present but too short or too long, meta descriptions that exist but do not meet length recommendations, pages with few internal links. These matter, but they should be addressed after the Red Zone is clear.

The Green Zone tab shows what your site is doing correctly. Reviewing this reminds you where your SEO foundation is solid and helps you focus effort on what actually needs improvement.

Screenshot: RankFrame SEO Audit with the Red Zone tab active, showing a list of critical issues with page names and issue descriptions

Speed Tab: Lighthouse Scores and Core Web Vitals

The Speed tab inside the site-wide audit surfaces Lighthouse performance data for both mobile and desktop. You see gauge-style scores alongside specific Core Web Vitals metrics: Largest Contentful Paint (LCP), Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS), and Total Blocking Time (TBT).

Mobile speed receives special weight because Google uses mobile-first indexing. A site scoring 90 on desktop but 45 on mobile is still being evaluated primarily on its 45-point mobile performance. The Speed tab surfaces this discrepancy clearly so you can investigate and address it.

Below the scores, RankFrame displays specific improvement suggestions sourced from the Lighthouse audit: "Serve images in next-gen formats," "Eliminate render-blocking resources," "Reduce unused JavaScript." Each suggestion includes an estimated impact in seconds of load time saved. For detailed guidance on reading the Speed tab, see the Speed Insights documentation.

Why Mobile Speed Matters More Than You Think Google's mobile-first indexing means the mobile version of your page is the primary version Google uses for ranking. Even if most of your site's visitors come from desktop, your mobile performance score is what Google is weighting. A beautiful Framer site with large hero videos and heavy animation may look stunning but score poorly on mobile performance, suppressing organic rankings for all device types.

Re-Analyze Button

After making fixes in Framer and republishing, click the Re-analyze button in the audit panel to refresh all checks and recalculate your score. This confirms that your changes had the intended effect and gives you an updated baseline for tracking ongoing progress.

A Step-by-Step Framer SEO Audit Workflow

Here is a complete audit workflow you can run on any Framer site, whether you are doing an initial launch audit, a maintenance audit on an existing site, or an investigation into unexpectedly flat organic traffic.

1

Run the site-wide audit and record your baseline score

Open RankFrame in Framer, navigate to the SEO Audit section, and click Analyze Site. Note your overall score and the breakdown of Red Zone, Warning, and Green Zone items. This is your baseline. Download the initial PDF before making any changes.

2

Fix Red Zone items first

Switch to the Red Zone tab and work through every critical issue. Each issue includes a description of the problem and what to fix. Common Red Zone items on new Framer sites include missing meta descriptions across multiple pages, missing image alt text site-wide, pages inadvertently set to noindex, and canonical tags pointing to preview URLs rather than live URLs. Fix each one in the Framer editor before moving on.

3

Run per-page audits on your top 10 pages

Identify your highest-value pages (homepage, service pages, key landing pages, top blog posts) and run individual per-page audits. Review all five sections. Pay particular attention to Basic SEO on pages that target your most competitive keywords. Use the AI-generated title and description suggestions where improvements are needed. See the Page Title and Description guide for best practices.

4

Address Warning-level issues on priority pages

Once Red Zone items are cleared, work through Warnings on your most important pages. These typically include title tag length optimization, meta description improvements, and internal linking enhancements.

5

Review the Speed tab and address performance issues

Open the Speed tab to review Lighthouse scores. If mobile performance is below 70, investigate the specific suggestions provided. Common Framer-specific performance improvements include compressing hero images, removing unnecessary Framer component animations, and reducing the use of video backgrounds.

6

Re-analyze to confirm fixes

After publishing your changes, return to the RankFrame SEO Audit panel and click Re-analyze. Verify that Red Zone items have moved to Green. Check your updated overall score. If items persist in the Red Zone, revisit those specific checks and confirm the fix was applied and published correctly.

7

Download the updated PDF and document progress

With improvements applied and confirmed, download a new PDF audit. Archive both the before and after PDFs as documentation of your SEO work. For client sites, present the comparison as a deliverable.

Ongoing Audit Cadence An SEO audit is not a one-time event. Set a calendar reminder to run a full site-wide audit at least once per month on active sites, and always run an audit after any significant update to site structure, navigation, or content. Framer site changes (adding pages, restructuring navigation, updating CMS templates) can introduce new issues that a fresh audit will catch immediately.
