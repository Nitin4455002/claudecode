Why Google Search Console Matters for Framer Sites

Framer is a visual web builder that outputs fast, clean, SEO-friendly HTML. But publishing your Framer site does not automatically mean Google is finding it, crawling it correctly, or indexing it completely. Without Google Search Console, you are flying blind: you have no visibility into which pages are indexed, which keywords are generating impressions, whether Google is encountering errors, or whether your sitemap is being processed.

Google Search Console (GSC) is a free tool from Google that gives you a direct line of communication with Google's search infrastructure. It shows you how Google crawls and indexes your site, which queries your pages appear for in search results, your click-through rates, and any technical issues Google has encountered. For a Framer site owner, this is the foundation of any SEO monitoring workflow.

What you get from a verified GSC property

  • Performance data: Clicks, impressions, average position, and CTR for every query and page over the past 16 months.
  • Coverage report: A breakdown of indexed pages, excluded pages, crawl errors, and redirect chains across your entire Framer site.
  • URL Inspection: On-demand crawl simulation for any URL, showing exactly what Google sees when it visits that page.
  • Sitemaps: Confirmation that your sitemap has been processed and how many URLs Google has discovered from it.
  • Enhancements: Reports on structured data (schema markup), Core Web Vitals, and mobile usability.
  • Manual Actions: Notifications if Google has applied a manual penalty to your site or specific pages.

None of this data is available without a verified GSC property. Every Framer site should be connected to GSC from day one of going live. If your site has been live for months without a verified GSC property, you have been missing this data the entire time and have no historical baseline for your search performance.

GSC data delay Google Search Console data has a 2 to 3 day delay. Performance data you see today covers searches that happened up to three days ago. Coverage and indexing data is generally more current and updates closer to real-time. Keep this in mind when reviewing data after making changes to your Framer site: you will not see the impact of a change in GSC until a few days after the change is live.

Verification Methods Available for Framer

Google offers several ways to verify ownership of a property in Search Console. Not all of them work well with Framer's architecture. Here is a comparison of the available methods and how they apply to Framer sites.

Verification Method Works With Framer? Difficulty Notes
HTML meta tag (URL prefix property) Yes, recommended Easy Paste meta tag into Framer Site Settings custom head code. No DNS access needed.
DNS TXT record (Domain property) Yes, recommended for domain-level coverage Medium Requires access to your domain registrar's DNS settings. Covers all subdomains in one property.
HTML file upload No N/A Framer does not support uploading arbitrary files to your site root. This method is not available for Framer-hosted sites.
Google Analytics Yes, if GA is already connected Easy Works if you have Google Analytics with the same Google account installed on your Framer site. Requires GA to be actively tracking.
Google Tag Manager Yes, if GTM is already installed Easy Works if GTM is already deployed on your Framer site via the custom head code. Requires GTM container to be active and publishing.

The HTML meta tag method is the starting recommendation for most Framer users because it requires no external tools and is entirely self-contained within Framer's Site Settings. The DNS method is preferable if you want to verify a Domain property (which covers both www and non-www, and all subdomains) rather than a specific URL prefix property. If you already have Google Analytics or Google Tag Manager on your Framer site, those are the fastest paths since verification is automatic once GSC detects those tools.

URL prefix vs. domain property When you add a property in GSC, you choose between a URL prefix property (for a specific URL like https://yoursite.com) and a Domain property (for your entire domain including all subdomains and both http and https). The Domain property requires DNS verification. The URL prefix property supports the HTML meta tag method. For most Framer sites hosted on a single domain, a URL prefix property for https://yoursite.com covers everything you need. If your site uses subdomains (like app.yoursite.com or blog.yoursite.com as separate Framer projects), a Domain property gives you a consolidated view.

Complete Step-by-Step Verification Walkthrough

Here is the complete process for verifying your Framer site in Google Search Console using the HTML meta tag method. You need: a Google account, your Framer site published with a custom domain, and access to the Framer editor.

1

Go to Google Search Console and Add a Property

Open search.google.com/search-console and sign in with your Google account. If you have no properties yet, you will be taken directly to the property setup screen. If you have existing properties, click the property selector dropdown in the top-left and choose "Add property."

You will see two options: Domain (requires DNS verification) and URL prefix. For the HTML meta tag method, select "URL prefix." Enter your Framer site's full URL, including the protocol. For example: https://yoursite.com. If your Framer site is set up to redirect www to non-www (or vice versa), enter the canonical version (the one that does not redirect). Click Continue.

Screenshot suggestion: Google Search Console's "Add property" screen showing the two property type options: Domain on the left and URL prefix on the right, with the URL prefix field filled in with an example Framer site URL.
2

Choose the HTML Tag Verification Method

After entering your URL, Google will show the Verify ownership screen. At the top, you may see a recommended verification method based on your Google account (often Google Analytics or Tag Manager if detected). Scroll past the recommended method to the "Other verification methods" section. Click on "HTML tag" to expand that option.

You will see a short explanation and a text box containing the full meta tag. This is the tag you will paste into Framer. Do not click Verify yet: you need to add the tag to your Framer site and publish first.

Screenshot suggestion: Google Search Console's verify ownership screen with the "HTML tag" section expanded under "Other verification methods," showing the full meta tag in the text box ready to be copied.
3

Copy the Meta Tag From Google Search Console

Click the copy icon next to the meta tag text box in Google Search Console, or manually select the full tag text and copy it. The tag will look like this:

<meta name="google-site-verification" content="abc123XYZexampleverificationcode" />

The content value is unique to your Google account and this specific property. Do not share this tag publicly, though it is low-risk since it only proves ownership of the site, not access to your Google account.

Make sure you copy the complete HTML tag including both angle brackets. If you only copy the content string (the alphanumeric value), pasting it into Framer will not work because Framer's custom code field expects complete HTML, not a raw string.

Leave this browser tab open. You will return to it after publishing your Framer site.

4

In Framer: Site Settings, Custom Code, Head Paste the Tag

Switch to your Framer editor. Click the gear icon in the top-left area of the editor to open Site Settings. In Site Settings, look for the "General" tab (it is usually the default view). Scroll down until you see the "Custom Code" section.

You will see two code input areas: one for code in the head of the page, and one for code before the closing body tag. Paste your Google verification meta tag into the head code input. The label may say "Start of <head> tag" or "End of <head> tag" depending on your Framer version. Either position works for the verification tag: Google only requires the tag to be present somewhere in the page head, not at a specific position within it.

If you already have other code in the head (Google Analytics, font preloads, other meta tags), paste the verification tag on a new line after your existing code. The order of tags in the head does not affect verification.

Screenshot suggestion: Framer Site Settings panel open with the Custom Code section visible, showing the head code input field containing the pasted Google Search Console verification meta tag.

Click Save or the equivalent confirmation button. Framer will register the change but it is not yet live on your published site.

5

Publish Your Framer Site

Click the Publish button in the top-right of the Framer editor. Wait for the deployment to complete. Framer deploys to its edge CDN, which is fast: most sites publish within 30 to 60 seconds. You will see a "Published" confirmation message when the deployment is complete.

This step is required before you can verify in GSC. Saving the custom code in Site Settings without publishing means the tag is only in your editor draft, not on your live site. Google's verification check fetches your live published URL, not your editor draft.

After publishing, quickly verify the tag is live by opening your site URL in a new browser tab, pressing Ctrl+U or Cmd+U to view the source, and searching for google-site-verification. Confirm the tag is present in the page source before proceeding to the next step.

6

Click Verify in Google Search Console

Return to the Google Search Console browser tab where you copied the meta tag. The verification screen should still be open. Click the Verify button.

Google will make a real-time request to your site's homepage to check for the presence of the verification meta tag. If the tag is found, GSC will show a green success message: "Ownership verified." Your property is now confirmed and GSC will begin collecting data for your site.

The initial data population in GSC takes time. Performance data (clicks, impressions, keywords) requires Google to have crawled and indexed your pages. Coverage data begins appearing within a day or two. You will not see 16 months of historical data on a new property: data collection starts from the moment verification is confirmed.

Verification successful: what happens next After successful verification, Google Search Console begins processing your site. The URL Inspection tool becomes available immediately for any URL on your site. The Coverage report populates over the next 24 to 72 hours as Google's systems process your property. Performance data starts building from your verification date, with the first meaningful data appearing within a week once Google has crawled your pages and recorded impressions.
7

Submit Your Sitemap

Inside your verified GSC property, go to the Sitemaps section in the left navigation sidebar. You will see a field to enter a sitemap URL.

Framer automatically generates and maintains a sitemap for every published site. The URL is always: yourdomain.com/sitemap.xml. For example, if your Framer site is myframersite.com, the sitemap URL is https://myframersite.com/sitemap.xml.

In the Add a new sitemap field, enter the full sitemap URL. You can enter either the full URL (https://yourdomain.com/sitemap.xml) or just the path (sitemap.xml). GSC accepts both formats. Click Submit.

GSC will show the sitemap status: Pending (Google is processing it), Success (processed successfully with a count of discovered URLs), or an error if the sitemap file could not be fetched. If you see a "Couldn't fetch" error, check that your Framer site is published and accessible at the sitemap URL by opening it directly in a browser.

Screenshot suggestion: Google Search Console Sitemaps section showing the sitemap URL field and a submitted sitemap with "Success" status and the number of discovered URLs displayed.

Once the sitemap is successfully submitted, Google will crawl the URLs in it and begin indexing your pages. This does not guarantee immediate indexing: Google decides which pages to index and when based on quality and relevance signals. Submitting a sitemap is a strong signal that you want these pages indexed, but it is a request, not a command.

More on the Framer Sitemap

Framer's automatically generated sitemap is worth understanding in detail before you submit it to GSC. Here is what it includes and what it does not.

What Framer includes in the sitemap

  • All published static pages with a public URL (pages not set to "no index").
  • All CMS collection item pages that are published and not excluded from indexing.
  • The sitemap uses standard XML format, and each entry includes the page URL and a lastmod date reflecting when the page was last published.

What Framer excludes from the sitemap

  • Pages where you have enabled the "No Index" setting in Page Settings. These are correctly excluded from the sitemap.
  • Pages with password protection.
  • Draft CMS items that have not been published.
Verifying your sitemap directly Before submitting your sitemap to GSC, open it directly in your browser at yourdomain.com/sitemap.xml. You should see a list of XML entries, one per page. Scan through the list to confirm all your important pages are present and no pages you want to exclude have accidentally been included. If the sitemap looks correct, you are ready to submit. If you see pages that should not be indexed, go to those pages' settings in Framer and enable the "No Index" option, then republish.

Sitemap updates after publishing new content

Framer updates the sitemap automatically every time you publish your site. When you add a new page or CMS item and publish, the sitemap URL is regenerated with the new entry. You do not need to resubmit the sitemap to GSC after adding new pages: Google recrawls submitted sitemaps periodically and will pick up new entries on its own schedule. However, if you want to signal new content urgently, you can use the URL Inspection tool in GSC to request indexing for the specific new page URL immediately after publishing.

What to Do After Verification

Successfully verifying your Framer site in Google Search Console is the start, not the finish. Here is the priority checklist for getting the most out of your newly verified GSC property in the first week.

Day 1: Sitemap and initial setup

  • Submit your sitemap at yourdomain.com/sitemap.xml as described above.
  • Use URL Inspection on your homepage to confirm it is accessible to Google (no noindex, no blocks in robots.txt).
  • Check the Coverage report for any immediate errors. A newly verified property will often show a small number of pages as "Discovered but not yet indexed" -- this is normal for new sites.
  • Set up email notifications in GSC Settings so Google can alert you to coverage drops, manual actions, or security issues.

Week 1: Review and configure

  • Check the Coverage report again. If pages are showing as "Excluded" unexpectedly, investigate: they may have noindex tags set, canonicals pointing elsewhere, or redirect issues.
  • Review the Enhancements section. If you have added schema markup to your Framer site (via RankFrame or manual code injection), the Enhancements tab will show any structured data errors for Google to detect.
  • Check the Manual Actions section (under Security and Manual Actions in the left nav). A clean site will show "No issues detected." If you see any actions listed, address them immediately.

Ongoing: Monitor and act

  • Review the Performance report weekly or monthly. Sort by impressions to see which pages are getting Google visibility, and by CTR to identify pages with ranking but low click rates.
  • When you publish major changes to your Framer site, use URL Inspection to request indexing of updated pages.
  • When you delete or rename pages in Framer, set up 301 redirects to ensure Google transfers any ranking signals to the new URL. Then use the Coverage report to confirm the old URL is no longer appearing as an error.

Key Google Search Console Reports for Framer Sites

Once your Framer site has been verified and data is collecting, these are the GSC reports that matter most for site owners and SEO practitioners.

Performance report

The Performance report shows you which queries your pages appear for in Google Search, how many impressions and clicks each query and page receives, and your average position. For a Framer site in its first few months, you will often find that you are getting impressions for keywords you were not specifically targeting. This is useful data for identifying content opportunities: pages that are getting impressions but ranking at position 10 to 20 are pages where modest SEO improvements could break into the top 5.

Filter the Performance report by page to see which individual pages are driving the most organic traffic. Sort by average position to find your near-miss rankings: pages in position 8 to 15 that could plausibly be moved to the first page with better on-page optimization.

Coverage report

The Coverage report categorizes all URLs Google has discovered from your site into four buckets: Error (indexing failed), Valid with warnings (indexed but with issues), Valid (indexed correctly), and Excluded (deliberately or automatically excluded). For Framer sites, the most common Coverage issues are:

  • Submitted URL blocked by robots.txt: A page in your sitemap is being blocked by Framer's robots.txt. Check if the page path is being excluded and whether that is intentional.
  • Duplicate without user-selected canonical: Multiple Framer pages have very similar content and Google cannot determine which is the canonical version. Verify your canonical tags are set correctly.
  • Crawled but not indexed: Google has visited the page but decided not to index it. This often indicates thin content, duplicate content, or a page Google considers low quality. Review these pages and either improve the content or add a noindex tag if the page is not intended for search.

URL Inspection tool

URL Inspection is the most direct way to diagnose why a specific Framer page is or is not appearing in Google. Enter any URL from your site, and GSC will show you Google's most recent crawl data for that URL: what the rendered HTML looked like, whether it is indexed, which canonical URL Google has selected, what structured data it detected, and whether there are any issues preventing indexing.

Use URL Inspection proactively after any significant change to an important page: after updating a page title or meta description, after adding schema markup via RankFrame, or after setting up a redirect from an old URL. The "Request Indexing" button within URL Inspection tells Google to prioritize recrawling that specific URL.

Troubleshooting Failed Verification

If you click Verify in GSC and get an error rather than a success message, here are the most common causes for Framer sites and how to fix each one.

Error: "Could not verify" or "Verification token not found"

This means Google fetched your page and did not find the verification meta tag in the head. Common causes:

  1. You did not publish after adding the tag. Saving in Site Settings is not the same as publishing. The tag must be on your live published site. Click Publish in Framer and wait for deployment to complete before verifying.
  2. You pasted the tag in the wrong place. The tag must be in Site Settings under Custom Code in the head section, not in an individual page's custom code. GSC checks the homepage root, and page-level custom code only applies to that specific page's output.
  3. You only copied the content value, not the full tag. The field in Framer expects complete HTML. Re-copy from GSC, making sure to select the complete <meta name="google-site-verification" ... /> tag including the angle brackets.
  4. CDN caching is serving the old page version. Wait 10 to 15 minutes after publishing and try again. Framer's CDN typically propagates quickly, but cache clearing can occasionally take a few minutes longer.

Error: "Your page may be unable to load"

This indicates GSC could not fetch your Framer site at all during verification. Check: Is your Framer site published and accessible? Is your custom domain properly connected to your Framer project? Try opening your site URL in an incognito window to rule out any local browser cache.

Verification succeeds but then loses ownership later

GSC periodically re-verifies ownership. If your verification meta tag is later removed from your Framer site (for example, if someone clears the custom head code in Site Settings), GSC will eventually send a warning email that verification cannot be confirmed and will then revoke the property ownership. Keep the verification tag in your Framer Site Settings permanently: it is a lightweight tag with no performance impact, and removing it risks losing your GSC property data.

Add multiple verification methods as a backup If the person who manages your Framer site ever changes the custom head code and accidentally removes the verification tag, you could lose access to your GSC property until you re-verify. Add a second verification method as a backup: if your domain registrar is accessible, add the DNS TXT record verification as a secondary method in GSC. GSC will accept either verification method, and having two active means losing one does not immediately revoke your property access.
