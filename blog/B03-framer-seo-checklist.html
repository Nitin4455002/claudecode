Section 1: Foundation (Items 1-7)

These seven items are the non-negotiables -- the structural and technical layer that everything else builds on. Complete these before anything else. A site with perfect meta tags but no sitemap submitted to Google is a site that may not get indexed at all.

Screenshot: Framer's Site Settings panel showing the Site Title, Description, and Favicon fields
    #1
    Set your site title and description

    In Framer's Site Settings (the panel accessible from the top-left menu), set a global site title and description. This acts as the fallback for any page that does not have individual meta overrides. Keep the site title under 60 characters. Write the description as a concise, compelling summary of what your site offers -- it will appear in search results when Google does not generate its own snippet.

    Easy -- Framer native
    #2
    Upload a favicon

    Upload a high-resolution favicon in Framer's Site Settings. Use a square image at minimum 512x512px in PNG format. Your favicon appears in browser tabs, bookmarks, and in some Google search result displays. It is a small signal that contributes to brand recognition and perceived trustworthiness.

    Easy -- Framer native
    #3
    Verify robots.txt is serving correctly

    Visit yourdomain.com/robots.txt and confirm the file loads without a 404 error. Framer generates this automatically. On the Pro plan, you can customize it to block specific paths (like preview URLs or internal admin paths) from being crawled. An incorrect robots.txt that accidentally blocks all crawlers is one of the most catastrophic and least visible SEO errors a site can have.

    Easy -- Framer native
    #4
    Confirm sitemap.xml is live and complete

    Visit yourdomain.com/sitemap.xml and verify that all pages you want indexed are listed -- including CMS collection pages (blog posts, case studies, products). Framer generates this automatically and updates it on every publish. If any important pages are missing, check that they are not set to noindex in their page settings.

    Easy -- Framer native
    #5
    Submit sitemap to Google Search Console

    Verify your site in Google Search Console using the HTML meta tag verification method: copy the meta tag from GSC, paste it into Framer's site-wide custom code head (under Site Settings > Custom Code), publish, then click Verify in GSC. Once verified, go to Sitemaps in the GSC sidebar and submit yourdomain.com/sitemap.xml. Without this step, Google discovers your pages only through organic crawling -- which is slower and less reliable.

    Medium -- requires GSC setup
    #6
    Confirm canonical tags are in place

    Use a browser inspector or an SEO browser extension (like Detailed.com's SEO extension) to check that each page includes a self-referencing canonical tag in the head section. It should look like: <link rel="canonical" href="https://yourdomain.com/page-slug" />. Framer adds these automatically on paid plans. If you have custom canonical needs (for syndicated content), override them via custom code in the page head.

    Easy -- Framer native
    #7
    Connect a custom domain

    Ensure your site is published on a custom domain (yourbrand.com) rather than a Framer subdomain (yoursite.framer.app). Subdomains can be indexed by Google, but a custom domain is essential for brand recognition, consistent link building, and long-term SEO equity. All backlinks should point to your custom domain, not to a platform subdomain that you might eventually migrate away from.

    Easy -- Framer native (requires paid plan)
Quick win: Items 1 through 6 can be completed in under 30 minutes on a new Framer site. Do them before you write a single word of content. Technical foundations that are wrong from day one are painful to fix retroactively.

Section 2: Page-Level SEO (Items 8-14)

On-page SEO is where most Framer sites are underoptimized. These seven items cover the per-page settings that Google reads first when evaluating your content. Many of these can be automated or AI-assisted using RankFrame's per-page SEO panel.

Screenshot: Framer's page settings panel with meta title and description fields highlighted, alongside RankFrame's AI-generated suggestions for the same page
    #8
    Write unique meta titles for every page (under 60 characters)

    Open each page's settings in Framer and write a unique, keyword-targeted meta title. Keep it under 60 characters to prevent truncation in search results. Prioritize your primary keyword near the front of the title. Avoid duplicating meta titles across pages -- duplicate titles confuse Google about which page is most relevant for a given query. For CMS-driven sites, map meta titles to CMS fields so every published item gets a unique title automatically.

    Faster with RankFrame AI
    #9
    Write unique meta descriptions for every page (150-160 characters)

    Write a meta description for every page between 150 and 160 characters. Include your primary keyword naturally and a clear value proposition. Meta descriptions do not directly affect rankings but significantly affect click-through rates from search results -- a well-written description can double CTR compared to a generic one. If you do not set a description, Google generates its own snippet from page content, which is often suboptimal.

    Faster with RankFrame AI
    #10
    Confirm exactly one H1 on every page

    Every page should have exactly one H1 heading. In Framer, add text and set its style to "Heading 1" (H1). The H1 should reflect the page's primary keyword. Use the browser inspector (View Source or DevTools) to verify there is only one H1 tag per page, especially on pages built with Framer's section templates, which sometimes include decorative large text that gets incorrectly styled as H1.

    Easy -- Framer native
    #11
    Use a logical H2/H3 heading structure

    Structure your page content with H2 and H3 headings in a logical, hierarchical order. H2s should be major section headers; H3s should be subsections within an H2. Avoid skipping heading levels (going from H1 directly to H3). Logical heading structure helps Google understand how your content is organized and can influence which sections appear as featured snippets or in AI-generated answers.

    Easy -- Framer native
    #12
    Set an Open Graph image for every page (1200x630px)

    Upload a custom OG image for each page or set a site-wide default. The recommended size is 1200x630px. This image controls how your page looks when shared on LinkedIn, Twitter/X, Slack, WhatsApp, and Facebook. A well-designed OG image significantly increases click rates on social shares. In Framer's page settings, the OG image field is typically labeled "Social Image."

    Easy -- Framer native
    #13
    Check no important pages are accidentally set to noindex

    In Framer's page settings, there is a search indexing toggle. Confirm that all pages you want indexed have indexing enabled. Also check any pages you intentionally excluded from the sitemap -- if they appear in Framer's sitemap.xml but have a noindex meta tag, you are sending contradictory signals to Google. Be deliberate: noindex any pages you genuinely do not want indexed (thank-you pages, internal preview pages), and index everything else.

    Easy -- Framer native

    Run a per-page SEO audit score

    Use RankFrame's per-page SEO audit to get a 0-100 score for each page and a list of specific issues to fix. Issues are categorized by severity so you know where to focus. Common findings: missing H1, meta description too short, no schema markup, images without alt text, thin content. Fix Red zone issues first, then Warning zone issues. This replaces the manual spreadsheet audit that most designers skip entirely.

Section 3: Schema Markup (Items 15-18)

Schema markup is the most commonly skipped SEO task on Framer sites, and the one with the highest potential upside. Rich results -- FAQ dropdowns, article bylines, star ratings, breadcrumb trails -- are only available to pages with correct schema. Framer has no native schema UI, so this section takes more effort than the others. RankFrame removes most of that friction.

Screenshot: Google search results showing a rich result with FAQ schema and breadcrumb trail from a Framer site using RankFrame
    #15
    Add Organization schema to your homepage

    Organization schema tells Google your business name, URL, logo, social profiles, and contact information. It is the foundational schema for any commercial website and directly feeds the Knowledge Panel that Google may show for branded searches. Use RankFrame's Schema Generation UI to fill in a form and inject this automatically, or write it manually as JSON-LD in Framer's custom code head. Required fields: @type, name, url, logo. Recommended: sameAs (social profiles), contactPoint, address.

    Much faster with RankFrame
    #16
    Add Article or BlogPosting schema to every blog post

    Every blog post should include Article or BlogPosting schema. This tells Google the article's headline, author, date published, date modified, and publisher. Article schema qualifies pages for the article rich result carousel in Google News and Google Discover, and provides structured author attribution that supports E-E-A-T (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness) signals. Use RankFrame's AI Auto-Generate Schema to apply this across your entire blog in one batch operation.

    Much faster with RankFrame
    #17
    Validate every schema with Google Rich Results Test

    After adding schema to any page, test it at search.google.com/test/rich-results or the Schema.org Validator. Enter your page URL and look for errors (red) and warnings (yellow). Common errors: missing required fields, incorrect date formats, typos in @type values. Fix all errors before publishing. Rich result eligibility requires clean, error-free schema -- Google will not show rich results for pages with schema errors.

    Medium -- external tool

    Explore additional schema types relevant to your content

    Beyond Organization and Article, consider schema types relevant to your specific content: Service schema for service businesses, Person schema for personal brands or author profiles, Restaurant schema for hospitality sites, Book schema for authors and publishers. Each type adds structured context that Google can use to qualify your pages for specific rich result formats. Section 4: Image SEO (Items 19-21)

Image SEO is undervalued on Framer sites, where visual design is central and images are everywhere. Missing alt text is the single most common SEO issue found in Framer site audits. These three items fix that and go further.

Screenshot: RankFrame's Image SEO tab showing a site-wide grid of images with green (alt text present) and red (alt text missing) status indicators
    #19
    Add descriptive alt text to every image on your site

    In Framer, you can set alt text for each image by selecting it in the editor and updating the Alt Text field in the properties panel on the right. Alt text serves two purposes: it helps screen readers describe images to visually impaired users (accessibility), and it tells Google what each image depicts (SEO). Use RankFrame's Image SEO tab to get a site-wide view of which images are missing alt text and use AI generation to fill them in bulk (1 credit per image). Descriptive, keyword-relevant alt text -- not keyword-stuffed strings -- is the goal.

    Much faster with RankFrame
    #20
    Compress images -- hero images under 200KB

    Large images are one of the most common causes of slow Largest Contentful Paint (LCP) scores. Before uploading images to Framer, compress them: hero images should be under 200KB, supporting images under 100KB. Use tools like Squoosh (squoosh.app), ImageOptim, or TinyPNG. Export images as WebP format where possible -- WebP files are typically 25-35% smaller than equivalent JPEGs at the same visual quality. Framer does not auto-compress uploaded images, so this step is entirely your responsibility.

    Medium -- external compression step
    #21
    Use descriptive, hyphenated filenames before uploading

    Rename image files before uploading to Framer. Use descriptive, lowercase, hyphen-separated names that reflect the image content: framer-seo-checklist-2026.webp rather than IMG_4821.jpg or image1.png. Google uses filenames as contextual signals for image indexing. Descriptive filenames contribute to Google Images ranking and provide additional keyword context for the page. This takes 10 seconds per image and is almost universally skipped -- which is exactly why it provides a marginal advantage.

    Easy -- pre-upload step

Section 5: Technical SEO (Items 22-24)

Technical SEO on Framer is largely handled by the platform -- but these three items require your active attention. Core Web Vitals, broken links, and 404 monitoring are the areas where Framer sites most often develop invisible issues that cost rankings without obvious symptoms.

Screenshot: RankFrame's Page Speed tab showing Lighthouse scores for a Framer site -- Performance, FCP, LCP, CLS for mobile and desktop
    #22
    Check Core Web Vitals: LCP under 2.5s, CLS under 0.1

    Run a Lighthouse audit on your most important pages using either RankFrame's Page Speed tab or Google PageSpeed Insights (pagespeed.web.dev). Focus on: Largest Contentful Paint (LCP, target: under 2.5 seconds), Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS, target: under 0.1), and Interaction to Next Paint (INP, target: under 200ms). Common issues on Framer sites: heavy scroll animations increasing CLS, unoptimized hero images increasing LCP, and third-party embed scripts increasing INP. Google uses Core Web Vitals as a ranking signal -- pages in the "Poor" zone face a ranking disadvantage against otherwise equivalent competitors.

    Faster with RankFrame Page Speed
    #23
    Find and fix broken internal links

    Broken internal links (links that point to URLs that return a 404 error) waste Google's crawl budget and create poor user experiences that increase bounce rate. Use a tool like Screaming Frog SEO Spider (free for up to 500 URLs) or RankFrame's 404 Monitor to identify broken links. Common causes on Framer sites: renaming a page slug without updating links that pointed to the old URL, deleting CMS items that other pages still reference, or copying content that included absolute links to old domain URLs.

    Monitor with RankFrame 404 Monitor
    #24
    Set up 404 error monitoring

    404 errors do not just affect current visitors -- they also affect how Google crawls your site over time. Repeated 404s from external links that used to point to valid URLs can cause Google to lose confidence in your site's reliability. RankFrame's 404 Monitor tracks every broken URL hitting your site with hit counts and user agent breakdown (distinguishing real user hits from crawler hits). Use the Exception Routes whitelist to filter out expected 404s (like favicon.ico requests from old browsers or webhook endpoints). Review monthly and fix or redirect high-hit broken URLs.

    Requires RankFrame
Animation warning: Framer's scroll and hover animations are one of the platform's biggest selling points -- and one of the most common causes of CLS and INP issues. If your Lighthouse CLS score is above 0.1, check whether animations that move elements on scroll are causing layout shifts during the initial page load. Consider using opacity-only animations (fade in/out) instead of position-changing animations (slide, move) for above-the-fold content.

Section 6: Indexing and Analytics (Items 25-27)

The final three items shift from setup to ongoing workflow. These are the monitoring and action items that separate sites that maintain rankings from sites that let improvements decay. Run these processes on a regular cadence, not just at launch.

Screenshot: RankFrame's Submit Indexing tab showing a list of URLs with "Submit to Google" buttons and GSC click/impression data alongside each URL
    #25
    Connect Google Search Console and monitor search performance

    Google Search Console is the authoritative source for how your site performs in Google search: which queries trigger impressions, which pages get clicks, average positions, and which pages have indexing errors. Connect GSC to your Framer site (HTML meta tag verification method), submit your sitemap, and then connect RankFrame's Submit Indexing feature to pull GSC data -- clicks, impressions, CTR, position -- into the Framer plugin panel. Review performance weekly. Pay particular attention to pages with high impressions but low CTR -- these pages rank but are not getting clicked, which usually means the meta title or description needs work.

    GSC data accessible in RankFrame
    #26
    Set up keyword tracking for your target keywords

    Add your primary target keywords to RankFrame's Keyword Tracker. Position data is pulled from Google Search Console and displayed with sparkline history charts so you can see at a glance whether each keyword is rising, stable, or declining. Also use the Keyword Research tab to check Search Volume, Keyword Difficulty, Competition, and CPC for keywords you are considering targeting -- and assign winning keywords to specific pages. Review keyword positions weekly. A keyword that drops 5 positions is a signal to review and update that page's content, not a reason to panic.

    Requires RankFrame
    #27
    Use Submit Indexing after every major content update

    Every time you publish a new page, update an existing page significantly, or push a batch of new CMS content (blog posts, case studies), use RankFrame's Submit Indexing feature to push those URLs to Google via the Indexing API. This tells Google to crawl and re-index those specific URLs immediately, rather than waiting for Googlebot's next scheduled crawl (which can take days or weeks for lower-authority sites). For time-sensitive content -- product launches, event pages, press releases -- this can mean the difference between appearing in search before or after the moment that matters.

    Requires RankFrame

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I set up SEO on Framer?

Start in Framer's Site Settings to set a global site title and description. Then go to each page's settings panel to set individual meta titles and descriptions. Connect a custom domain, submit your sitemap to Google Search Console, and verify GSC ownership using the HTML meta tag in Framer's custom code head. For schema, image alt text, and keyword tracking, install RankFrame from the Framer Plugin Marketplace.

Does Framer automatically create a sitemap?

Yes. Framer automatically generates sitemap.xml at yourdomain.com/sitemap.xml. It includes all static pages and CMS collection pages and updates automatically every time you publish your site. Submit this URL in Google Search Console to ensure Google indexes your content promptly.

How do I add schema markup to a Framer site?

You have two options. Option one: write JSON-LD schema manually and paste it into the custom code head block in Framer's page settings. Option two: use RankFrame's Schema Generation feature, which provides a form-based UI for creating and injecting schema without writing code. RankFrame supports Organization, Article, BlogPosting, Service, Person, Restaurant, and Book schema types. Always validate schema using Google's Rich Results Test after adding it.

How do I check Core Web Vitals for my Framer site?

Use Google PageSpeed Insights (pagespeed.web.dev) to run a Lighthouse audit against your published Framer URL. Check Performance score, LCP (target: under 2.5s), CLS (target: under 0.1), and INP. You can also use RankFrame's Page Speed tab, which runs the same Lighthouse tests for both mobile and desktop directly inside the Framer editor without switching tools.

How do I verify my Framer site in Google Search Console?

In Google Search Console, choose the HTML tag verification method. Copy the meta tag GSC provides. In Framer, open your site settings, navigate to the custom code head section, and paste the meta tag there. Publish your site, then click Verify in Google Search Console. Once verified, submit your sitemap.xml URL in the Sitemaps section.

What is the ideal meta title length for Framer pages?

Keep meta titles under 60 characters to avoid truncation in Google search results. Include your primary keyword near the beginning of the title. For branded pages, the format "Primary Keyword | Brand Name" works well. Use RankFrame's AI meta tag feature to generate on-brand, keyword-targeted titles quickly for each page.

Can I do keyword research inside Framer?

Yes, with RankFrame installed. RankFrame's Keyword Research tab lets you check search volume, keyword difficulty, competition, and CPC for any keyword from within the Framer editor. You can also assign keywords to specific pages and track their position history using the Keyword Tracker feature with sparkline history charts.

How often should I run an SEO audit on my Framer site?

Run a full site SEO audit at least monthly, and after any significant site changes -- new sections added, pages reorganized, CMS content bulk-updated. RankFrame's Site-wide SEO Analyzer makes this fast. For ongoing monitoring, keep 404 tracking and keyword tracking active continuously so you catch issues between formal audits. Export the PDF audit report quarterly for documentation or client reporting.
