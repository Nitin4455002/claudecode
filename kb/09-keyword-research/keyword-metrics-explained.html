Keyword Research

Keyword Metrics Explained

When you click Check on any keyword in RankFrame, four metric cards populate below the input field. Each card tells you something different about how that keyword performs in search. Understanding what each number means helps you choose keywords that are realistic to rank for and valuable when you do.

The Four Metrics at a Glance

Each metric answers a specific question about a keyword. Read all four together rather than acting on any single number in isolation.

1
Search Volume (Search Vol)

The average number of times per month people search for this keyword globally. Volume is the first filter: if nobody searches for a term, ranking for it delivers no traffic regardless of how well you optimize.

A volume of 100 to 1,000 can be very worthwhile for niche Framer audiences where the visitors are highly targeted. Volumes above 10,000 are competitive by default and usually come with high KD scores. Match your volume expectations to your site's current domain authority: a newer Framer portfolio targeting 200 monthly searches is far more realistic than chasing 50,000.

Volume figures are global estimates and can fluctuate month to month based on seasonal trends or industry news. Use the number as a directional signal, not an exact forecast.

Search Vol metric card showing an example monthly search figure
2
Keyword Difficulty (KD)

A score from 0 to 100 estimating how hard it is to rank on the first page of Google for this keyword. The score factors in how many authoritative sites already rank for the term and how strong their backlink profiles are. A site with few backlinks competing against established domains with thousands of links will struggle at high KD, no matter how good the content is.

KD Range Label What it means for your site
0 to 29 Easy New sites can compete. Prioritize these terms early.
30 to 49 Moderate Achievable with strong, well-structured content and some backlinks.
50 to 69 Hard Requires meaningful domain authority. Plan for a longer timeline.
70 and above Very Hard Realistic only for established sites with strong link profiles.

Saved keywords with a high KD score show a red KD badge in the Saved Keywords list as a persistent visual reminder that these terms need more work to yield results.

KD metric card alongside the KD difficulty range reference
3
Competition

Reflects advertiser competition in Google Ads for this keyword. The value ranges from 0 (no advertisers bidding) to 1 (maximum advertiser competition). This metric comes from paid search data, not organic ranking data, so it is a different signal from KD.

High competition is a useful proxy for commercial intent. When many advertisers are bidding on a term, it typically means people searching for that term are buyers or decision-makers rather than casual browsers. That makes organic ranking for high-competition keywords more valuable: you are reaching people closer to a purchase or conversion decision even though you are not paying for the click.

A competition score of 0.7 or above generally indicates strong commercial intent. A score near 0 suggests the keyword is informational or niche, with fewer advertisers seeing a direct conversion value from it.

Competition metric card with a value near 0.75 as an illustrative example
4
CPC (Cost Per Click)

The average price advertisers pay for one click on a Google Ad targeting this keyword, displayed in USD. CPC is a direct measure of commercial value: advertisers only keep paying high CPCs when those clicks generate returns. That means a high-CPC keyword signals that each visitor who arrives via that search term tends to be worth something to a business.

Consider two keywords side by side. Keyword A has a CPC of $5 and 500 monthly searches. Keyword B has a CPC of $0.10 and 5,000 monthly searches. If you rank for Keyword A, every organic visit carries the implied commercial weight of a $5 click. If you rank for Keyword B, each visit is worth a fraction of that. Total potential value can be higher for the lower-volume, higher-CPC term.

Use CPC to prioritize which pages deserve the most optimization effort. Pages targeting high-CPC keywords often deliver a stronger business impact per ranking position gained.

Reading the Four Metrics Together

Each metric is more useful in context with the other three. The goal is to find keywords where the opportunity is real (volume), the path is realistic (KD), and the reward is meaningful (competition, CPC).

The ideal combination for most Framer sites, especially newer ones, looks like this:

  • Search Vol: 200 to 3,000 monthly searches (specific enough to be targeted, large enough to matter)
  • KD: under 40 (competitive enough to be valuable, achievable without massive domain authority)
  • Competition: 0.4 or above (indicates some commercial intent behind the searches)
  • CPC: above $1 (confirms each click has real business value)

A practical example helps illustrate the contrast. Suppose you are building a Framer agency site and you check two keywords:

Comparison Example
"framer website design"
Vol 1,200 KD 34 Comp 0.62 CPC $3.80
Strong target
"website design"
Vol 90,500 KD 82 Comp 0.91 CPC $5.20
Too competitive
"free design tips"
Vol 480 KD 18 Comp 0.09 CPC $0.15
Low value intent

In this example, "framer website design" is the strongest target. The volume is meaningful, the KD is achievable, and the CPC confirms commercial intent. "Website design" has impressive volume but a KD of 82 puts it out of reach for most Framer sites. "Free design tips" is easy to rank for, but the low CPC and competition score reveal that searchers are not in a buying mindset, making it less valuable even if you rank.

Practical rule: Start with KD as your filter. Remove any keyword above your site's current competitive level. Then rank the remaining options by CPC. The highest-CPC keyword that clears your KD threshold is usually your best starting point.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I always pick the highest search volume keyword?

Not necessarily. High volume keywords usually have high KD. A lower-volume keyword with a KD under 30 often delivers faster ranking results for a newer Framer site, and faster results mean faster traffic growth.

What does a KD of 0 mean?

It means very few or no authoritative sites are currently ranking for that exact keyword. This is an opportunity, but also worth verifying that the volume is real and not just a niche term nobody actually searches. Check the volume alongside the KD before committing.

Is CPC the same as what I would pay if I ran Google Ads?

CPC in RankFrame is the average benchmark across all advertisers. Your actual ad cost would vary based on your Quality Score, bid strategy, and competition at the time of the auction. Use CPC as a relative signal of value, not as a precise budget figure.

Why is competition shown as a decimal (e.g. 0.78) instead of a percentage?

It follows the standard Google Ads format where 0 is no competition and 1 is full competition. Multiply by 100 if you want to think of it as a percentage. A value of 0.78 is equivalent to 78% competition.

Can I use these metrics to decide which pages to optimize first?

Yes. Check the current keyword for each important page and compare KD and volume. Prioritize pages that target lower-KD keywords with reasonable volume since those have the fastest path to ranking improvement and measurable traffic gains.
