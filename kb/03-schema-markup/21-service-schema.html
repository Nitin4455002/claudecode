Service Schema

Service schema tells Google what service you offer, who provides it, where it is available, and how much it costs. Use this schema for agencies, studios, consultants, and any service-based business with a Framer website. This guide covers every field in the RankFrame Service schema form.

What service schema does

Service schema describes a service offering to Google. It provides structured information about what the service is, who offers it, the geographic area it covers, contact details, and pricing. Google uses this data to understand service-based businesses and match them with relevant queries in local and general search results.

This schema type is ideal for Framer sites representing agencies, design studios, consulting practices, SaaS companies, legal firms, financial advisors, and any business whose primary offering is a service rather than a physical product.

All form fields explained

The Service schema form contains eight fields organized into two groups: service details and contact information.

Service Details

Field What to enter
Service Name The name of the specific service you are marking up (e.g. "Brand Identity Design", "SEO Consulting", "Web Development"). If you have multiple services, create a separate schema entry for each one. Becomes the name property.
Description A 1 to 3 sentence description of what the service includes, who it is for, and what outcome it delivers (e.g. "We design brand identities for early-stage startups. Each project includes logo, color system, typography, and brand guidelines."). Becomes the description property.
URL The canonical URL of the page describing this service (e.g. https://yourdomain.com/services/seo). Becomes the url property.
Service Type The category or type of service. Use a clear descriptive label (e.g. "Web Design", "Digital Marketing", "Legal Consulting", "Financial Planning", "Software Development"). Becomes the serviceType property.
Provider Name The name of the person or organization providing this service (e.g. "7 Seers" or "Jane Smith Consulting"). This links the service to an entity. Becomes provider.name.
Area Served The geographic region where this service is available (e.g. "New York", "United States", "Europe", "Worldwide", "Remote"). For purely digital or remote services, use "Worldwide" or "Remote". Becomes the areaServed property.

Contact and Pricing

Field What to enter
Telephone The contact phone number for this service, including country code (e.g. +1-555-234-5678). Becomes provider.telephone. Leave empty if you do not accept phone inquiries.
Price The price for the service as a number (e.g. 1500 for a flat rate, or the starting price). For variable pricing, use the base or starting price. Becomes offers.price.
Currency The ISO 4217 three-letter currency code for the price (e.g. USD, EUR, GBP, INR, AUD). Must match the currency of the Price field. Becomes offers.priceCurrency.
Tip

If your service pricing is custom or quote-based, you can leave the Price and Currency fields empty. The schema remains valid without pricing data. Including a starting price when possible gives Google more context and can improve listing appearance.

Note

For agencies or studios with multiple service offerings (e.g. Brand Design, Web Development, SEO), create a separate Service schema entry for each service and bind each to its own page URL.

Saving and injecting

  1. Click the Preview JSON tab to review the complete JSON-LD output for this service.
  2. Click Validator (Ext) to open Google Rich Results Test and confirm the schema is valid.
  3. Click Copy JSON if you want a copy of the raw JSON-LD.
  4. Click Save Schema to save it to the Manual sub-tab in Saved Schemas.
  5. Toggle the schema on in Saved Schemas, then click Save and Inject.

Frequently asked questions

Who should use Service schema?
Any business or freelancer that offers a service. This includes agencies, design studios, consultants, software companies, marketing firms, law firms, and any other service-based business with a Framer website.
What should I enter for Service Type?
A clear descriptive label for the category of service. Examples: Web Design, SEO Consulting, Brand Strategy, Software Development, Legal Services, Financial Consulting.
What should I enter for Area Served?
The geographic region where you provide your service. This can be a city, state, country, or a general description like Worldwide or Remote. For fully remote services, use Worldwide.
What currency format should I use?
Use the ISO 4217 three-letter currency code: USD for US dollars, EUR for euros, GBP for British pounds, INR for Indian rupees. The Price field takes a number and Currency takes the code.
