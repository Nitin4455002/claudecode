What article schema does

Article schema tells Google that a page contains editorial content written by a specific author and published by a specific organization. When present and valid, it enables rich results including article carousels, author attribution, and top stories eligibility in Google Search.

This schema type is particularly valuable for Framer blogs, case study pages, and editorial sections. Adding complete article schema with author and publisher data strengthens Google's understanding of content origin and credibility.

The four article subtypes

At the top of the Article schema form is an Article Type selector. Choose the subtype that best describes your content:

  • Article: general editorial content. Use this when none of the other subtypes apply precisely.
  • BlogPosting: a blog entry or written post. Use this for individual posts on a Framer blog or journal section.
  • TechArticle: technical documentation, tutorials, developer guides, or how-to content aimed at a technical audience.
  • NewsArticle: a news story, press release, or announcement intended to report on recent events.
Tip

For most Framer blog pages, BlogPosting is the right choice. For product documentation or help articles, use TechArticle.

Article Type, Headline, URL, Description

Basic Fields
Field What to enter
Article Type Select from the four subtypes: Article, BlogPosting, TechArticle, or NewsArticle. This sets the @type property in the JSON-LD output.
Headline The title of the article as it should appear in structured data. Keep it under 110 characters. Match it closely to the visible heading on the page. Becomes the headline property.
URL The canonical URL of this article page (e.g. https://yourdomain.com/blog/my-post). Becomes the mainEntityOfPage identifier.
Description A brief summary of the article, ideally 1 to 2 sentences. This is not the meta description but the structured data description. Becomes the description property.

Image Details

Image Details

Google requires at least one image in article schema for rich result eligibility. The Image Details section has three fields:

Field What to enter
Image URL A direct URL to the article's featured or hero image. Use a full absolute URL (e.g. https://framerusercontent.com/images/...). Becomes the image.url property.
Image Width The pixel width of the image (e.g. 1200). Google recommends images at least 1200 pixels wide for standard rich results. Becomes image.width.
Image Height The pixel height of the image (e.g. 630). Use the actual dimensions of the file to keep schema accurate. Becomes image.height.
Note

Google recommends a 1.91:1 aspect ratio for article images. 1200 x 630 is the standard. Images below 1200 pixels wide may not qualify for rich result features.

Author Details

Author Details

The Author Details section identifies who wrote the article. Google uses this for authorship attribution and E-E-A-T signals.

Field What to enter
Author Name The full name of the individual who wrote the article (e.g. "Jane Smith"). Becomes author.name. If the author is an organization rather than a person, use the organization name.
Author URL A URL for the author's profile, bio page, or personal website. This helps Google connect the author name to a verifiable online identity. Becomes author.url.

Publisher Details

Publisher Details

The Publisher Details section identifies the organization that published the article. This is usually your brand, studio, or company.

Field What to enter
Publisher Name The official name of the publishing organization (e.g. "7 Seers" or "Acme Design Studio"). Becomes publisher.name.
Publisher Logo URL A direct URL to the publisher's logo image. Google prefers a rectangular logo with a white or transparent background, ideally 600 x 60 pixels or similar proportions. Becomes publisher.logo.url.

Dates

Dates

Both date fields are important for Google's freshness signals and rich result display.

Field What to enter
Date Published The date the article was first published. Use ISO 8601 format: YYYY-MM-DD (e.g. 2026-05-02). Becomes datePublished.
Date Modified The date the article was last updated. If the article has never been edited since publication, use the same date as Date Published. Keep this up to date when you revise the content. Becomes dateModified.

Saving and injecting

  1. Click the Preview JSON tab to review the complete JSON-LD output before saving.
  2. Click Validator (Ext) to open Google Rich Results Test and confirm the schema is valid for rich result eligibility.
  3. Click Copy JSON if you want a local copy of the raw JSON-LD.
  4. Click Save Schema to save to the Manual sub-tab in Saved Schemas.
  5. Toggle the schema on in Saved Schemas, then click Save and Inject.

Frequently asked questions

Which article subtype should I choose?
Use Article for general editorial content. Use BlogPosting for individual blog entries. Use TechArticle for technical documentation or tutorials. Use NewsArticle for news stories or press releases.
What image dimensions should I use?
Google recommends at least 1200 x 630 pixels. Enter the actual pixel width and height in Image Width and Image Height so the schema is accurate.
Does article schema affect CMS pages in Framer?
Each schema entry is bound to one specific URL from the sitemap. For CMS collection pages, create individual schema entries for each post URL you want to cover.
What date format should I use?
Use ISO 8601 format: YYYY-MM-DD. For example, 2026-05-02. This is the format Google expects in JSON-LD date fields.
