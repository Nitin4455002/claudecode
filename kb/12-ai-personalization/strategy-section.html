Strategy Section

The Strategy section is the second half of the Personalization tab. It has four fields: Main Goals, Industry, Audience, and Competitors. Together they shape the AI's tactical decisions: which keywords to favor, what tone to use, what comparison framing to apply. Where Brand Identity tells the AI "who you are," Strategy tells it "what you are trying to achieve and against whom."

Filling the Strategy Fields

1
Main Goals
A multi-select dropdown showing options like Increase Traffic, Generate Leads, Boost Sales, Brand Awareness, Local Presence, Improve Authority. Click to open the dropdown, then check each goal that applies. The header shows your current count (e.g. "4 Goals Selected"). The AI uses goals to bias copy: a "Generate Leads" goal pushes AI to write conversion-oriented descriptions. A "Local Presence" goal emphasizes location in alt text and schema.
Main Goals dropdown open with multiple options selected
2
Industry (Tag Input)
A tag input field. Type an industry name and press Enter to add it as a tag. Common entries: SaaS, Agency, E-commerce, Startup, Healthcare, Finance, Education. You can add multiple tags if you operate across industries. The AI uses Industry to choose vocabulary appropriate to your sector (e.g. SaaS-specific phrasing for SaaS sites).
Industry tag input with several tags added
3
Audience (Tag Input)
A tag input field similar to Industry. Type an audience descriptor and press Enter. Examples: SaaS Founders, Business Owners, Premium Businesses, Marketing Teams, Indie Hackers, Local Service Customers. Be specific. "People" or "everyone" is useless to the AI. "B2B SaaS founders running early-stage startups" gives the AI precise targeting for tone, vocabulary, and pain points.
Audience tag input with audience descriptors
4
Competitors URLs
A multi-line input where you add competitor URLs one per line (e.g. https://dashdigital.studio/). Click the "+ Add Competitor" link to add a new line. Include 3 to 5 direct competitors. The AI uses competitor URLs to understand your positioning context: it does not copy competitor copy, but it uses competitor positioning as a reference for differentiation in your generated meta descriptions.
Competitors URL list with Add Competitor link
5
How the Four Fields Work Together
The AI combines all four signals to produce contextual output. A site with "Generate Leads" + "SaaS" + "SaaS Founders" + 3 competitor URLs gets AI output tuned for SaaS-founder lead generation, with positioning that differentiates from the listed competitors. Filling all four fields is significantly more powerful than filling one or two.
6
Save Settings
The full-width purple Save Settings button at the bottom of the Personalization tab saves both Brand Identity and Strategy in one action. Changes take effect on the next AI generation. There is no separate save for the Strategy section.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I select more than one Main Goal?
Yes. Main Goals is a multi-select. Choose every goal that applies. Two to four selections is typical. Selecting all six dilutes the signal: prioritize.
Do Competitor URLs need to be active websites?
Yes. The AI may inspect public meta data from competitor URLs to understand positioning. Broken or private URLs provide no signal. Use the live homepage URL.
What if my industry is very niche?
Add a specific tag (e.g. "Pet Care SaaS" or "Wedding Photography Agency") even if it is not on a standard list. Tags are free text after you press Enter.
Can I add competitor URLs that are not on Framer?
Yes. The competitor list is for positioning context, not platform compatibility. WordPress, Webflow, Squarespace, custom-coded sites all work as competitor references.
How does Audience differ from Industry?
Industry is the vertical you operate in (e.g. "SaaS"). Audience is who buys from you (e.g. "SaaS Founders"). Both can apply: a SaaS Industry might serve an Audience of "Engineering Leaders" and "Product Managers". Fill both for the strongest AI signal.
